1. Model architecture relevant to the new wing model
Separate load paths
The report computes aerodynamic forces and moments caused by the rotor wake separately from the forces and
moments caused by freestream flow. This is the key architectural point: wake and freestream regions are spatial regions
with different local flow, not two complete aerodynamic laws blended by flight speed.
Wake-immersed wing region
For the portion of the wing inside a rotor wake, the model determines the immersed area, local angle of attack, and local
dynamic pressure before computing lift and drag.
Freestream remainder
The wing area outside the wake is evaluated with the freestream local-flow quantities. The two area contributions are
then combined as physical area integrals.
Large negative incidence
At low speed, rotor wake can drive the wing to large negative incidence. The report states that lift and drag tables are
defined to approximately 90 degrees in magnitude for this regime.
Asymmetric operation
Left and right wakes are treated separately. Unequal wake coverage during asymmetric flight naturally produces roll and
yaw moments.
Project implication
The replacement wing model should use one common full-angle coefficient database for every strip. Wake and
freestream portions may differ through local velocity, angle of attack, Reynolds number, Mach number, dynamic pressure,
covered area, and moment arm. They should not be represented by a speed-dependent blend of complete FNear and
FLiftLine force/moment results.
2. Minimum mathematical form
For each left/right wake and each wing strip, define the wake-covered area SiW and local flow state. A modern
implementation consistent with the report structure can be written as:
qiW = 0.5 rho |ViW|2
LiW = qiW SiW CL(alphaiW, ReiW, MiW, delta)
DiW = qiW SiW CD(alphaiW, ReiW, MiW, delta)
Mc/4,iW = qiW SiW c Cm(alphaiW, ReiW, MiW, delta)
The freestream part uses Sfree = Sstrip - SiW and the same coefficient law evaluated at the freestream local state. Total
strip force and moment are the sum of the physical area contributions.
These equations are a clean modern restatement of the report architecture. They are not claimed to reproduce every symbol or empirical
constant in the 1973 implementation.

4. Source limitations that must remain visible
• The 1973 model was constrained by real-time computing limits and used simplified rotor aerodynamics and uniform
inflow.
• The rotor-wake/airframe interaction model relied on limited powered-model data.
• The induced-velocity constants were specific empirical fits, not universal tiltrotor constants.
• The report itself recommends extending airframe aerodynamics to plus/minus 180 degrees and improving above-stall
downwash representation.
• A modern full-angle wing database should therefore use CR-114614 for structural guidance and geometry definitions,
while using XFOIL, TM-88373, and explicit post-stall extensions for the coefficient data chain.
